What changed

Completion improved materially

18.6% → 75.3%

Weighted completion rose by 56.7 percentage points after replacing Kiln with independently graded greenfield products, fixing harness defects, and tuning model/agent behavior.

  • Small, medium, and large tasks carry weights 1, 3, and 8.
  • The comparable baseline is evaluator-normalized; the originally recorded baseline was 18.0%.
  • Seven of nine selected model/task cells fully passed.

Qwen completed the whole ladder

12/12 · 14/14 · 18/18

Qwen completed all three products. Its large QueuePilot run survived five real Pi compactions and passed every external acceptance check, but required 200 turns and 7.54 million Pi-accounted tokens.

  • Large wall time: 24.2 minutes.
  • Mean TPOT: 13.4 ms; no vLLM waiting requests or preemptions.
  • A second earlier configuration also reached 18/18, while one repeat was stopped after 35 minutes.

Serving capacity was not the limiter

0 queued · 0 preempted

Selected runs showed low KV-cache pressure, strong prefix-cache reuse, and no scheduler queuing. Failures came from agent decisions, ambiguous contracts discovered in the harness, generated application behavior, and excessive tool/model work—not GPU scarcity.

  • Selected large-run KV peaks were 0.6% to 7.9%.
  • Prefix-cache hit rates were 88.8% to 92.4% on selected large runs.
  • The dominant optimization target is useful work per turn, not larger KV allocation.

Muse is efficient but variable

92.6% weighted

The retained Muse prompt passed LinkLedger and HookBin and reached 16/18 on QueuePilot. A prior candidate reached 17/18, while repeats ranged from 14 to 17, showing meaningful behavioral variance.

  • Selected large run: 8.0 minutes and 299,857 Pi-accounted tokens.
  • Remaining final failures were an incorrect missing If-Match status and a dashboard placeholder containing the tenant token.
  • Literal HTTP-contract guidance fixed the earlier replay and oversized-body failures.

Gemma needs a lifecycle fix

2 full passes · 1 deadlock

Gemma passed the small and medium products after evaluator normalization. Its final QueuePilot implementation reported nine internal tests passing, but the independent evaluator hung twice, including a separate five-minute diagnostic run.

  • The agent itself exited normally after 17.0 minutes.
  • The result is conservatively scored 0/18 because independent completion could not be established.
  • This is a generated-product reliability failure even though the harness classified the missing evaluator result as infrastructure_error.

Cold-start storage matters

~35 min to all services ready

The RHEL image and services worked, but model staging and compilation over the mounted workspace were slow. Bare metal should place model snapshots, compilation caches, and hot benchmark workspaces on local NVMe.

  • Bootstrap required a retry and finished about ten minutes after pod creation.
  • Model prefetch/loading took roughly four to seven minutes per service.
  • Compilation and CUDA graph capture added roughly five to six minutes per model.

Why attempts failed

Reasoning without delivery

Baseline Qwen exhausted its response budget discussing the problem and often produced no usable implementation. Disabling thinking and requiring an implementation-first workflow converted this into full passes.

  • Baseline Qwen scored 1/12, 0/14, and 0/18.
  • The final large run still needed five compactions, so controller efficiency remains a target.

Almost-correct HTTP behavior

Muse repeatedly built useful products but substituted semantically plausible behavior for exact contracts: socket destruction instead of a 413 JSON response, replay metadata with the wrong field shape, and 400 instead of 409.

  • The retained suffix tells Muse to honor status and field contracts literally.
  • Draining oversized request bodies before responding fixed health-after-rejection behavior.

Generated process lifecycle deadlock

Gemma QueuePilot passed its own tests but the external evaluator never emitted a result. A separate diagnostic evaluator remained stuck for five minutes, making the generated service unsafe to count as completed.

  • Likely areas are lingering handles, shutdown behavior, SSE connections, or an unbounded request path.
  • Future evaluation should capture active handles and per-check deadlines.

Evaluator over-specification

The HookBin evaluator required a literal form tag even though the public contract required a usable interface. Qwen and Gemma had working input/button UIs and were wrongly failed. The evaluator now tests observable usability instead.

  • Reference fixtures and empty implementations still validate 14/14 and expected failure.
  • Both baseline and optimized reporting are normalized to the corrected criterion.

Hidden response-shape assumptions

Some QueuePilot and HookBin checks expected exact event names or response shapes that were not fully stated. Those contracts were published in the task specifications before the final runs.

  • The benchmark now measures implementation, not guessing an evaluator convention.
  • The change improved interpretability but means pre- and post-fix attempts are not pure model-only A/B tests.

Token and turn amplification

Qwen served tokens much faster than Muse or Gemma, yet its large pass consumed over 25 times Muse's selected tokens. Faster decoding alone did not guarantee a faster or cheaper completed task.

  • Qwen large: 7.54M tokens, 200 turns, 24.2 minutes.
  • Muse large: 0.30M tokens, 22 turns, 8.0 minutes, 16/18 checks.

Technical strategy

  1. 1

    Freeze a reproducible v1

    Tag this commit, preserve the paid artifact archive, pin model revisions and package versions, and record image digests. Re-run the selected nine cells on bare metal before changing anything else.

    Exit gate: All services pass health checks and selected scores reproduce within declared tolerance.

  2. 2

    Harden the evaluator boundary

    Give every acceptance check its own deadline, capture open sockets/handles when a check stalls, and guarantee cleanup in finally blocks. Classify evaluator failures separately from generated-product deadlocks while still scoring unverifiable products as incomplete.

    Exit gate: Synthetic leaking and hanging fixtures terminate predictably with diagnostic evidence.

  3. 3

    Optimize agent work efficiency

    Measure useful accepted checks per turn, million tokens, tool call, and minute. Add loop detection for repeated reads/tests, adaptive test selection, and a controller that asks for implementation or verification when progress stalls.

    Exit gate: Qwen large remains 18/18 with at least a 30% reduction in turns or tokens across three seeds.

  4. 4

    Tune compaction deliberately

    A/B Pi compaction thresholds and summaries on the large task. Preserve requirements, failing checks, changed-file state, and the next action; measure post-compaction rework and repeated commands.

    Exit gate: No acceptance regression and lower duplicate work across at least three runs per configuration.

  5. 5

    Run a controlled vLLM matrix

    Hold model prompts and task seeds constant while comparing FP8 versus automatic KV dtype, prefix caching on/off, context ceilings, batch-token limits, and Qwen speculative-token counts. Optimize completion first, then time and energy.

    Exit gate: Select settings using paired runs with completion as a hard gate and report confidence intervals.

  6. 6

    Add multi-agent long-running scenarios

    Introduce planner/implementer/reviewer roles sharing a repository, with conflicting edits, handoffs, resumptions, and 4–8 hour tasks. Track coordination messages, duplicate work, merge conflicts, compactions, and recovery after injected tool failures.

    Exit gate: The stack completes two multi-agent products without manual intervention or cross-tenant/state leakage.

  7. 7

    Establish promotion gates

    Require repeated full completion, bounded p95 wall time, no hidden evaluator assumptions, no orphan processes, and a cost-per-success ceiling. Keep best-observed and final-retained profiles separate.

    Exit gate: A candidate is promoted only after three consecutive full passes per critical task and a documented rollback path.

Bare-metal transfer plan

  1. 1

    Transfer and verify artifacts

    Copy the archive to the bare-metal host and verify SHA-256 before extraction. Keep the original archive immutable; extract into a versioned experiment directory.

    Exit gate: The recorded and locally computed SHA-256 values match.

  2. 2

    Recreate the RHEL runtime

    Build the repository Containerfile or mirror its pinned RHEL 10.2 packages. Install NVIDIA driver/toolkit versions compatible with the host, Node.js 24.22.1, Pi Coding Agent 1.2.0, and vLLM 0.31.0.

    Exit gate: Version inventory matches the report and all three GPUs pass CUDA health checks.

  3. 3

    Stage models on local NVMe

    Place Hugging Face snapshots, vLLM compile caches, and active benchmark workspaces on local NVMe instead of NFS. Use a durable slower volume only for copied results and backups.

    Exit gate: All three services reach readiness without network-filesystem stalls and cold-start timing is recorded.

  4. 4

    Launch one model per GPU

    Bind Qwen to GPU 0 on port 8001, Muse to GPU 1 on 8002, and Gemma to GPU 2 on 8003. Preserve BF16 weights, FP8 KV, 65,536 context, 0.92 memory utilization, 16 sequences, 16,384 batched tokens, prefix caching, and Qwen MTP=3.

    Exit gate: Health/model endpoints and Prometheus metrics are reachable for every service.

  5. 5

    Install and validate ForgeBench

    Checkout the reported commit, run npm ci where lockfiles apply, then run npm run check. Keep reference solutions inaccessible to the agent execution user.

    Exit gate: Reference fixtures pass 12/12, 14/14, and 18/18; empty fixtures fail.

  6. 6

    Run a no-cost smoke and selected replay

    Exercise Pi JSON mode, shell timeout injection, metric snapshots, artifact permissions, and one small task per model before the nine-cell reproduction.

    Exit gate: Telemetry is complete, no credentials are captured, and processes shut down cleanly.

  7. 7

    Promote with rollback

    Version the vLLM unit files, Pi configuration, prompts, and model revisions together. Keep the prior stack launch files and model symlinks so rollback is a service restart, not a rebuild.

    Exit gate: The reproduced completion and efficiency results meet the promotion gates.

Method, boundaries, and caveats

Task design and scoring

LinkLedger, HookBin, and QueuePilot are created from empty seeded projects and graded by independent black-box tests. Per-model completion is the weighted mean of task fractions with weights 1, 3, and 8; the overall result is the mean across models.

Hardware and software

The paid pod used RHEL 10.2, three NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs, vLLM 0.31.0, CUDA 13, Node.js 24.22.1, Pi Coding Agent 1.2.0, and one model service per GPU.

Model assignment

GPU 0 served Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B, GPU 1 served Muse-Glimmer-30B, and GPU 2 served Gemma-4-31B-it. Qwen used three-token MTP speculative decoding; all services used the same core cache and batching limits.

Baseline normalization

The initial HookBin evaluator over-specified a literal form tag. The usable-interface criterion was corrected and validated against references and empty fixtures. The displayed baseline credits Gemma 13/14 rather than the originally recorded 12/14; the original aggregate was 18.0%.

Selection policy

The capability matrix uses the retained final configuration where possible, not an oracle assembled solely from the best score. Muse final large is 16/18 even though an earlier candidate achieved 17/18. Corrected medium grades replace known false UI failures.

Token interpretation

Pi-accounted totals include repeated prompt/context processing and are work indicators, not unique natural-language tokens. vLLM prompt and generation counters plus prefix-cache metrics are retained in each run.

Budget boundary

The campaign used one three-GPU RunPod plus a prior aborted allocation, under the approved $100 ceiling. A separate watchdog was installed to terminate the pod at the budget deadline if the controlling session failed.

Statistical limits

The campaign was optimization-oriented rather than a fully powered model comparison. Large-task repeats revealed substantial stochastic variance, so future claims should use at least three fixed seeds per configuration and report distributions.
